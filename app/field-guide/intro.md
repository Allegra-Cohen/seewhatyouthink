
The NYC and LA school districts ban generative AI. Stanford University launches a Cognitive Security Task Force. The tech billionaires are waxing rapturous about a world in which intelligence is as available as electricity...but we can't tell if that's sinister or not. Underneath fears about rogue AI, labor market collapse and gradual disempowerment, there's a deeper worry: As machines become more capable, will humans be able to keep thinking for ourselves? And what does that really mean, anyways?

It's a complicated question, but we have answers. The Field Guide to Thinking for Ourselves is here to take you on a tour of all the problems, solutions, domains and people associated with thinking in the age of AI. For AI novices and neutrals, the Guide will help you decide when, how and why you want to engage with generative AI. For AI safety folks, we're trying to convince you that thinking for ourselves underpins many other concerns like gradual disempowerment and loss of control. And for funders, we'll be assembling a map of the work that needs to be done so that humans can still learn, create, decide and coordinate even as AI advances. It's no big deal, but thinking for ourselves determines everything from the strength of democracies to resisting the machine empire. So grab your hiking shoes and let's get a move on!

The Guide is machine-assisted but 100% human curated and written. We aim to add new parts every other Wednesday.
