
The NYC and LA school districts ban generative AI. Stanford University launches a Cognitive Security Task Force. The tech billionaires are waxing rapturous about a world in which intelligence is as available as electricity...but we can't tell if that's sinister or not. Underneath fears about rogue AI, labor market collapse and gradual disempowerment, there's a deeper worry: As machines become more capable, will humans be able to keep thinking for ourselves? And what does that really mean, anyways?

It's a complicated question, but we have answers. The Field Guide to Thinking for Ourselves will take you on a tour of all the problems, solutions, domains and people associated with thinking in the age of AI. For AI novices and skeptics, the goal is to get you comfortable with the lingo and able to form your own opinions, even as the AI discourse gets louder. For AI safety folks, the goal is to get you to think a little bit more about the human systems in which AI operates, and maybe come out of it feeling a little more empowered. And for funders, well, the goal is to help you avoid funding things that damage human autonomy and cognitive agency, and start funding things that help humans learn, create, decide and coordinate even as AI advances. It's no big deal, but thinking for ourselves underpins everything from the strength of democracies to resisting the machine empire. So no matter what you do in your day job, this Guide should have a little something for everyone!

The Guide is machine-assisted but 100% human curated and written. We aim to add new parts every other Wednesday.
